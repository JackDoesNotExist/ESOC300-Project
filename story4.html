Third Month of Treatment


A good chunk of my treatment was spent trying to find a medicine that would cut down the nausea I was feeling. The nausea really sucked, which I think is why it's one of the first things I remember when looking back at my treatment. It didn't feel like regular nausea, like what you feel on a car ride or something comparable. There was truly nothing I could do to lessen the nausea. None of the usual remedies, like ginger ale, water, or watching your posture, helped. Thankfully, we did eventually find a medication that helped with that nausea, though it was towards the end of my treatment.


After completing my treatment plan, I had to get scans done again to check and see if the cancer was gone. The scans were annoying, but they weren't the absolute worst. However, I did have to fast the night and morning before the scans. That on its own sucked, but to add to it, the scans were early, so I had to go to bed earlier on those nights before the scans. One of the scans I had to get was a PET/CT scan. This was the most annoying test because they had to inject me with a contrast dye to highlight my lymph nodes. But the dye needed time to work its way through my system. That meant that I had to sit and wait for a few hours while that happened. I tried to sleep, but that only worked to a point, especially because I was hungry.


Thankfully, the scans all showed that I was cancer-free. My official remission date is March 23rd, 2021, and I've been in remission for 5 years now. The whole ordeal was so quick in the grand scheme of things, and throughout all of it, I was still just living my life like nothing was happening. Playing video games, doing online school, hanging out with my friends. My mom said that she remembered getting home from a particularly rough appointment and talking to my dad about it while I was just casually singing in the shower. And honestly? I don't even remember that appointment.
