Second Month of Treatment


I was diagnosed in December, during winter break. That means I was in school while receiving treatment. However, if there was one good thing that came from COVID, it was online schooling. The reason that was good was that I didn't have to be at school physically. I was already tired from chemotherapy, so I was glad to be able to just have my camera off when my teachers allowed it. Speaking of teachers, Banner had a teacher meet with me and ask if I needed any help with my classes or if I needed her to speak with any of my teachers at school. They also gave me an iPad to KEEP to use so I could work on my classes while receiving treatment. Honestly, the remainder of my treatment kind of all blended together. The thing that always stayed constant was the nausea. The nausea is the biggest thing I remember from treatment. Now, I only felt it while actually receiving treatment and not outside of that, but it still sucked.


Going through all this during COVID was kind of rough because only one parent was allowed to be with me during appointments where we met with the oncologist. We had to wear masks to the appointments, which, while necessary, was annoying when I didn't even want to be there in the first place. The appointments were often early in the morning as well, so that definitely didn't help. The appointments were essentially check-ins. They would take my blood to check my counts and ask how I was feeling. It was also a good opportunity to ask questions. Well, for my parents to ask questions. They'd always ask, "Do you have any questions, Jack?" I'd just shake my head and go, "Nope." It was always the same. Definitely one of the funnier moments.
